*This is the twelfth in a weekly series on my DC sabbatical (Sep 1 → Nov 30, 2026). Catch up on [earlier posts](https://mitchradakovich.com/blog/) if you missed them, and [join my mailing list](mailto:mitch@allaboardohio.org?subject=Add%20me%20to%20your%20mailing%20list&body=Please%20add%20me%20to%20your%20mailing%20list.) for future posts.*

Scott and I pulled into the cell phone lot at the Portland, Maine airport at 3:30 in the morning and napped for about an hour before returning our rental car. We had just driven five hours from a concert in New Brunswick, Canada. I had a 6am flight, and by that evening I would be giving a speech on a train platform in central Pennsylvania. Yes, you read that right, and yes, Scott and I are insane.

Let me explain how we got there.

Landing back in DC after the ground stop saga (see [Week Eleven](https://mitchradakovich.com/blog/week-eleven-in-dc-when-life-gives-you-ground-stops/)), I met Scott on the Mall to make the most of the daylight we had left, and we took a bike ride around the Capitol. While he checked out the Library of Congress, I hopped on a quick call with Luke Radel, a college friend of my sister Megan who has been making quite a name for himself in DC as a reporter. He asked both Trump and Vance questions at a press conference the other day!

*cabi.png

After the call, Scott and I went to the Air and Space Museum, which I hadn’t visited since the renovations (really nice!). We dropped our stuff at the house and biked over to a fantastic dinner at Chercher. It was Scott’s first time eating Ethiopian food, and I’m pleased to say it blew him away. On the way home we grabbed snacks for a very long train ride.

Our train left DC at 6am and wound through Baltimore, Philly, New York and all of Connecticut before depositing us in Boston, where our friends Nicole and Kaylie were waiting. We walked around the Seaport, a neighborhood that is developing so fast it is mind-boggling. What I will say about it is that it is cool to see legitimately good architecture in new apartment and condo buildings. They aren’t just plain rectangles there, which I think is how it should be everywhere.

*boston.png

After dropping our stuff at Nicole and Kaylie’s place, we took a driving tour through Cambridge. We stopped at [Ward Maps](https://mbtagifts.com/pages/mbtagifts-at-the-wardmaps-retail-store), an incredible MBTA and antique map store that I could have spent hours in, and then had dinner at [Oleana](https://www.oleanarestaurant.com/), a Michelin-recommended Turkish fusion tapas spot. When I say every bite was mind-blowing, I am not exaggerating. We wound down back at the house listening to music, including Leif Vollebekk (more on him soon...).

Nicole and Kaylie’s neighborhood, Jamaica Plain, was next on the list. It has a main street lined with local businesses, from groceries and pizza to barbers and vets, and it felt like a true community. We grabbed breakfast sandwiches and walked around the neighborhood park before Kaylie dropped Scott and me at the T. We spent a quick hour downtown, including a stop at the beautiful Boston City Hall. (I am not even kidding. I know lots of folks hate it, but brutalism is so cool.) We grabbed cannolis in the North End before boarding our next train, up to Portland, Maine.

*cannoli.png

That train, the Downeaster, is a real inspiration to me for Ohio. It is a state-supported route that has grown in both frequency and ridership since it launched. A well-run authority, the [Northern New England Passenger Rail Authority](https://www.nnepra.com/), oversees it, and it has so much success to show for itself. This is exactly the kind of service we could have connecting Ohio’s cities.

In Portland we picked up a rental car and checked out a couple of lighthouses in the sliver of daylight we had left (it was fully dark by 4:15). Then we checked into our hotel and started a brewery crawl. For the beer lovers out there, Portland is a heck of a spot. We hit four or five breweries, with poutine and a sandwich as intermission. At our last stop we were joined by Pat, our bartender from the first one, who filled us in on all the drama in town. Needless to say, it was a great night. For anyone looking for recommendations, check out Austin Street and The Send.

The drive up the Maine coast made for one of the longest days of my life, and I wouldn’t change a thing. Scott and I spent the whole day stopping whenever something looked interesting. We had huge lobster rolls for lunch and explored small towns, lighthouses and a lot of beautiful nature. As the sun set we reached the Canadian border, which was a milestone for Scott, who had never left the US before!

*lighthouse.png

Two more hours of driving got us to Saint John, New Brunswick, for the [Leif Vollebekk](https://open.spotify.com/artist/3jzXlBF2157k4exx7idecs?si=aPjCBD4hQ_uaCzH2UD-nyQ) concert. This man is a genius. He can play seemingly every instrument, and both his lyrics and his music are incredible. I highly recommend checking him out. Think of a combination of Bon Iver and Noah Kahan. After the show we grabbed Tim Hortons to fuel up for the five-hour drive back to Portland, which brings us back to that cell phone lot.

I had planned to sleep on my first flight to DC, but I ended up in a long and deep conversation with my seatmate instead. (In true Mitch Radakovich fashion. I promise I didn’t start it!!) DC was my layover, where I brushed my teeth, changed, and gave myself a quick sink shower to look as presentable as possible before my connecting flight to Pittsburgh.

Bill Gray, one of the most frequently recurring characters on this blog, picked me up at the airport along with Kevin Moore from Western Pennsylvanians for Passenger Rail. We headed straight to Pittsburgh’s Amtrak station and jumped right into the thick of it. It was launch day for the second daily Pennsylvanian, a new round trip between Pittsburgh and New York.

And what an event it was!! Shout out to Margaret Clark and the full Amtrak team for fantastic logistics. There were tons of great people to talk with before the speeches, including Amtrak’s Interim President Byl Herrmann. When I introduced myself, he said, “I know who you are! I love when All Aboard Ohio pops up on my Instagram.” That is SO COOL! It was also great to catch up with Amtrak, Norfolk Southern, FRA and community leaders I have met over the past few months, including Art Guzzetti, who I had just met in DC a week earlier.

*byl.jpg

The speeches came from Byl, Amtrak Board Member Robert Gleason and Pennsylvania Governor Josh Shapiro, all from behind a podium labeled “All Aboard Pennsylvania.” Very interesting to see the parallels with AAO ;)

After the speeches we boarded the last car of the train, an Amtrak Metroliner complete with a conference room and phone booths. These are the cars used to carry dignitaries, and it was an incredible experience to ride in one with so many influential people. The ride itself was beautiful, and at every stop crowds of people were celebrating the new train, with mayors, senators, marching bands and hundreds of community members.

I got off at Huntingdon, PA, where I gave a talk about the future of the route and the connections between Pennsylvania and its neighbors, including Ohio. You can watch the speech [here](https://www.facebook.com/reel/1152961157192641) and see the news coverage [here](https://www.wtaj.com/video/a-second-daily-train-now-connects-pittsburgh-and-nyc/12385363/).

*huntingdon.png

After my speech we grabbed dinner at a local spot and went back to the station to wait for the next train to Pittsburgh. While we waited, we met Flora, who opens the Huntingdon station for the community every day. She has been doing this nearly every day for the past 35 years. She only misses two days a year, when she visits her son in Pittsburgh for Thanksgiving. She was such a light on an already wonderful day, and a fantastic reminder of how central a train station can be to a community.

Back in Pittsburgh, Bill dropped me off at my airport hotel (thanks, Bill), and I crashed. Luckily I set three alarms first, because I slept through the first two. My flight to Baltimore was at 5:30am. Why do I keep doing this to myself???

Before catching the train from BWI back to DC, I grabbed a coffee and had a wonderful call with Thomas Abelman to dig deeper into a conversation we started a few weeks ago. He gives great advice, and he recommended I focus more on outputs and impact in my pitch. Edits are in progress!

I finally made it home and changed into loungewear almost immediately. Still, the grind doesn’t stop. I had a great catch-up with Bill Hutchison and Ed D’Amato from the Lakeshore Rail Alliance, and then we met with Andy Kunz and Ezra Silk from the [US High Speed Rail Association](https://www.ushsr.com/). (Not to be confused with last week’s High Speed Rail Alliance! USHSR represents the companies and unions in the space.) We talked about where our strategies overlap, and it went so well that we scheduled a follow-up with their team for the next day, where I walked them through our AAO and national network strategy and we had a great brainstorm on next steps.

I also had an overdue reconnect with Deborah Fischer Stout from Northern Flyer Alliance in Kansas. I love these calls because we are working on so many of the same problems, and they are a constant reminder of the value of collaboration across statewide organizations. Speaking of which, this week I received two more requests for help from advocates in other states, Pennsylvania and Louisiana, who want to grow advocacy back home. Bring on the Statehouse Rail Network!

The last event of the week was a great in-person catch-up with Luke (as mentioned earlier). Megan has been saying that we would be friends, and man was she right. It was the perfect way to close out one of the busiest weeks of my sabbatical.

*luke.png

No book report this week, I fear. Between trains, planes, rental cars and a $10 couch, the reading took a back seat.

As I write this, the email backlog has grown, so I am buckled down working my butt off. The sabbatical is drawing to a close, and while I feel like I have achieved nearly all of the goals I laid out in my first post, there is still so much work to be done, from more fundraising to helping our teams implement AAO’s new organizational design. I also need to figure out what is next for me. I’ll dig into all of that in a bigger reflection post next week.

Next up is the move home. I am renting a car and heading back to Cincinnati this week ahead of Thanksgiving, and I start back at P&G the following week. Although this sabbatical is nearing its end, my journey is not. I am going to keep writing this blog, maybe a little less often, so I can keep all of you wonderful supporters updated as I keep navigating this crazy but wonderful life.
